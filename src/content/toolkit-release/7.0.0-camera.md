## Breaking Changes

To fix certain bugs, and re-align features to ensure they behave the same across all platforms, we did have to modify some APIs and the associated underlying behavior. Please find the features you use, below, and update your apps accordingly.

<details><summary>All packages</summary>

- **API:** **Null arguments throw `ArgumentNullException` at the call.** Public and protected members of the five libraries now guard the non-nullable reference parameters they cannot handle (about 275 guards).
  - A null that used to cause a `NullReferenceException` later (or was stored silently) fails at the call, with a `ParamName`. What to do: fix the caller.
  - An async member throws from the call itself, before it returns a Task.
  - Null arguments that a member handled before are still accepted, for example an `IProgress<double>` or a `Paint` background of null.
  - Feature-specific effects are listed in each feature's section.
  - `BasePlatformBehavior` guards only `bindable`. A null platform view passes through, because the Android `IconTintColorBehavior` accepts it.
- **Setup:** **CommunityToolkit.Maui.Core on Android adds a `<queries>` entry** for the launcher (MAIN/HOME) to the app's merged manifest through the package's library manifest (`src/CommunityToolkit.Maui.Core/AndroidManifest.xml`). Badge needs it on Android 11 and later. If your app or Play listing reviews `<queries>`, check the merged manifest.
- **Setup:** **Dependencies:** on Windows, CommunityToolkit.Maui.Core references `System.Speech` 10.0.11 (it was 10.0.1). No action is needed unless you pin the package. This comes from #3288 on main, not from this PR.

</details>

<details><summary>API</summary>

<details><summary>Behavior</summary>

- **Failures are reported through events instead of exceptions.**
  - `StartCameraPreview(CancellationToken)` no longer throws for a camera that cannot start (except in the Android failed-connection case below). It raises `ErrorOccurred` on every platform.
    - A cancelled token still throws `OperationCanceledException`.
    - A view without a handler still throws `InvalidOperationException`.
  - The commands let no exception escape:
    - `CaptureImageCommand`, `StartVideoRecordingCommand` and `StopVideoRecordingCommand` report through `MediaCaptureFailed`.
    - `StartCameraPreviewCommand` and `StopCameraPreviewCommand` report through `ErrorOccurred`.
  - Failures of the handler's own work (connecting, switching to `SelectedCamera`, applying `ImageCaptureResolution`) raise `ErrorOccurred` instead of ending the app.
  - Android:
    - A preview without the camera permission now fails, is reported, and stays stopped until the activity resumes with the permission. iOS and Mac Catalyst report a denied permission the same way.
    - `CaptureImage` before the camera has started throws `CameraException` instead of waiting until its token is cancelled.
  - What to do: subscribe to `ErrorOccurred` and `MediaCaptureFailed` instead of relying on try/catch.
- **Android: one `ErrorOccurred` for a failed connection.** A `StartCameraPreview` called while the `CameraView` connects to its camera waits for the connection.
  - When the connection fails and raises `ErrorOccurred`, that `StartCameraPreview` throws the same failure to its caller instead of raising `ErrorOccurred` again. `StartCameraPreviewCommand` doesn't report it again.
  - What to do: catch the exception where you await such a start.
- **`StopCameraPreview` cancels starts that still wait.** `StopCameraPreview` and `StopCameraPreviewCommand` now cancel a pending `StartCameraPreview`, a pending `StartVideoRecording` (both overloads), the start the view makes when it connects its camera, and a camera switch.
  - The cancelled start throws `OperationCanceledException`, with no `ErrorOccurred` or `MediaCaptureFailed`. A start that a stop interrupted needs a new start.
  - What to do: do not treat that exception as a failure.
  - On iOS and Mac Catalyst, `StartCameraPreview` with an already-cancelled token throws and no longer starts the session.
- **Video recording.**
  - Android: a `StartVideoRecording` during a recording replaces it: the new stream receives the video, and the earlier recording's video is discarded. Camera 6.1.0 ignored the second start; iOS and Mac Catalyst already replaced it.
  - Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows: a start that arrives while an earlier start still waits for the platform joins it. It returns once that recording has started, its stream receives no video, and it throws the earlier start's failure. On iOS and Mac Catalyst this holds for a double tap too, where Camera 6.1.0 replaced the first recording.
  - `StartVideoRecording` returns only once the platform has started the recording (#3311), so `await` it before relying on the recording. On iOS and Mac Catalyst, a start cancelled while it waits releases the recording state.
  - Windows, as on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst:
    - `StopVideoRecording` waits for a pending `StartVideoRecording`.
    - A `StartVideoRecording` or a second `StopVideoRecording` that arrives while a stop waits for Media Foundation to finish the recording waits for that stop.
  - `StopVideoRecording` throws `CameraException` on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst when the platform reports a recording with no playable video. A recording the platform ended early that has usable video is still returned.
  - Android: when the photo session cannot be restored after a successful export, `StopVideoRecording` still returns the video, and the failure is raised through `ErrorOccurred`. Selecting another camera during a recording waits until the recording stops or is replaced.
  - Windows: a `StopVideoRecording` whose token is cancelled already or while the stop finishes the video:
    - It still stops the recording and throws `OperationCanceledException`, as on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst. It used to ignore the token and return the video.
    - The next `StopVideoRecording` returns the video. A `StartVideoRecording` that comes first discards it and puts its stream back, as for a replaced recording.
    - The camera that the recording turned on is off again when the cancelled stop throws.
    - A cancelled stop while a start or another stop is still pending throws without stopping anything.
    - With no recording, a cancelled `StopVideoRecording` returns `Stream.Null` on every platform.
    - `StopVideoRecordingCommand` ends without an event.
    - Camera 6.1.0 already handled a cancelled stop this way on Android and iOS; Mac Catalyst follows iOS.
    - Catch the `OperationCanceledException` and call `StopVideoRecording` again to get the video.
  - Windows: a `StartVideoRecording` during a recording ends and finishes the running recording and discards its video.
    - The running recording's stream goes back to the length and position it had when that recording started.
    - The video goes to the stream of the latest start, as on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst. The camera stays on in between.
    - Camera 6.1.0 prepared a second recording without ending the first, so the first video was left unfinished or the new stream stayed empty.
    - If you relied on the first recording continuing, call `StopVideoRecording` before a new start.
  - What to do: handle `ErrorOccurred` and `CameraException`. On Android, don't rely on a second start being ignored.
- **Recording while the preview is stopped starts the camera.**
  - On Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows, `StartVideoRecording` after `StopCameraPreview`, or before `StartCameraPreview`, starts the camera and then the recording. Android already did; Camera 6.1.0 recorded nothing on iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows.
  - A camera that the recording turned on turns off again when the recording ends. After a cancelled `StopVideoRecording` on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst, it turns off only once the next stop returns the video.
  - A preview that the app started stays on.
  - When the camera can't start, a stopped preview stays stopped. On iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows, `StartVideoRecording` throws the failure: for example a `CameraException` without a camera or without the camera permission, or on Windows an `UnauthorizedAccessException` when the privacy settings block the camera. `StartVideoRecordingCommand` reports it through `MediaCaptureFailed`.
  - On Android, a `StartCameraPreview` after `SetExtensionMode` on a stopped preview shows the `SelectedCamera`. Camera 6.1.0 kept showing the camera that was selected when `SetExtensionMode` ran.
- **Windows: a start while the preview runs, and a stop during a recording.**
  - `StartCameraPreview` while the preview already runs is a no-op.
  - A `StartCameraPreview` while an earlier start is still opening the camera releases that start's `MediaCapture`, so the camera is never held twice.
  - `StopCameraPreview` during a recording ends the recording, like on Android. A later `StopVideoRecording` returns `Stream.Null`, so do not expect a video from a recording that a stop ended.
